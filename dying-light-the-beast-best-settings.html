Dying Light: The Beast: best graphics settings

Updated: October 8, 2026

Dying Light: The Beast (2025) gives you a long list of graphics options, but only a few of them decide whether you hit your frame-rate target. Below are the best-looking settings that still hold 60 FPS on four common PCs, from an RTX 3060 at 1080p up to an RX 9070 XT at 4K, in the same order as the in-game menu. Ray tracing is left off in these presets; turn it on only if you have frame-rate headroom.

Which settings to lower first

These settings give the biggest frame-rate gains when you lower them:

  • Volumetric Fog & Clouds: up to +11% FPS
  • Reflections: up to +8% FPS
  • Effects & Particles: up to +5% FPS
  • Post-Processing: up to +5% FPS

Compared with the RX 9070 XT build, the RTX 3060 build lowers 4 settings: Volumetric Fog & Clouds (Medium → Low), Foliage (High → Ultra), Effects & Particles (Medium → Low), Post-Processing (Medium → Low). Everything else can stay where it is.

Frequently asked questions

Can the RTX 4060 run Dying Light: The Beast at 1080p 60 FPS?

Yes. With the settings in this guide the estimate is about 62 FPS at 1080p, above the 60 FPS target.

Which setting costs the most FPS in Dying Light: The Beast?

In our calculations Volumetric Fog & Clouds gives the biggest single gain when lowered (up to +11% FPS), followed by Reflections.

How much VRAM does Dying Light: The Beast need?

With the settings in this guide it uses about 7.2 GB at 1080p on an RTX 4060 and about 11.7 GB at 4K on an RX 9070 XT.
