CPU or GPU bottleneck? How to tell what is holding you back

By the FPSmith team · Published: October 20, 2026

The same RX 9070 XT with three different processors in Counter-Strike 2, Fortnite, Cyberpunk 2077 and Flight Simulator 2024, plus how to spot a bottleneck yourself.

Every frame involves two jobs: the processor prepares the game logic, physics and the commands for the graphics card, and the graphics card draws the image. Whichever is slower decides your frame rate. That is a bottleneck, and every system has one; what matters is which part it is, and in which game.

Same graphics card, three processors

In the FPSmith settings tool we paired an RX 9070 XT at 1080p with three processors: a Ryzen 5 3600 from 2019, a Ryzen 5 5600 from 2022 and the Ryzen 7 9800X3D, one of the fastest gaming processors.

Two lessons. First, in competitive games and simulators the processor is everything: with the same graphics card, Counter-Strike 2 nearly doubles. Second, in graphics-heavy games like Cyberpunk 2077 the gains stop after a certain point; the Ryzen 5 5600 and the 9800X3D are only 3 FPS apart because the graphics card is now the limit.

How to spot a bottleneck yourself

  • Lower the resolution. If the frame rate barely moves, the processor is the limit. If it rises clearly, the graphics card is.
  • Watch graphics card usage. With an in-game overlay (such as the performance overlay in the NVIDIA or AMD app), usage that stays below about 95% usually means the processor cannot keep up.
  • Watch busy scenes. City centres, large fights and many characters load the processor. If frame rates only drop in those moments, you are processor-bound.

Is a bottleneck bad?

No. A graphics card bottleneck is actually what you want: you are using all of the card you paid for. The problem is when the processor stops you short of your goal, for example a 240 Hz monitor with a processor that cannot go past 150 FPS.

What settings can do

Graphics card limited: Global illumination, volumetrics, shadows and upscaling all help. Anything tied to resolution gains frames.

Processor limited: Most graphics settings make little difference. Crowd density, object and view distance, physics and in some games shadow distance help. Upscaling barely helps at all.

Why X3D processors are so good for games

AMD's X3D processors have a very large cache. Games keep touching the same data, so a large cache means the processor waits on main memory less often. That is the reason for the big gaps in the table.
