Which graphics settings cost the most FPS? We calculated it for 43 games

By the FPSmith team · Published: October 20, 2026

Global illumination, volumetrics, shadows, textures: the settings that cost the most frames across 43 story games, and what they do to the image.

In most games the difference between "everything Ultra" and "everything High" is hard to see, but the frame-rate difference can be huge. The catch is that most of that difference comes from a handful of settings. Know which ones are truly expensive and you can leave the rest at maximum.

The study: 43 games, RTX 4060, 1080p, 60 FPS

Using the FPSmith settings tool, we looked at 43 story-driven games targeting 60 FPS at 1080p on an RTX 4060 with a Ryzen 5 7600. For each game we recorded the gap between "everything on maximum" and "the best-looking settings that hit the target", and the single setting with the biggest gain when lowered.

One more result stands out: across these 43 games, well-chosen settings deliver on average about 1.5 times the frame rate of maximum settings, while most settings still stay at their highest level.

1. Global illumination

It calculates how light bounces off surfaces and fills a scene. In Unreal Engine 5 games it appears as "Global Illumination" or Lumen and is the single most expensive setting. Dropping it one step usually makes shadows look a little flatter indoors, but the frame-rate difference is large. In many of the games we looked at it alone was worth more than 20%.

2. Volumetric effects

Fog, clouds and light shafts. The visual difference between High and Ultra is often just how soft their edges are. Dropping them to Medium or High is one of the least visible cuts you can make.

3. Texture quality: a special case

Texture quality normally barely affects frame rate; it uses video memory (VRAM). That changes when memory runs out. That is exactly why texture quality was the top gain in 6 games: the RTX 4060's 8 GB is not enough for those games' highest textures. On an RTX 3060 with 12 GB, the same games can keep textures at maximum. See Is 8 GB of VRAM enough? for details.

4. Reflections and shadows

Screen-space reflections and distant shadows are expensive, especially in open worlds. Lowering reflections one step means a little less detail in puddles and windows. With shadows, the difference between High and Ultra is usually just how sharp distant shadows are.

Settings that are almost free

Some settings can nearly always stay at maximum because they cost very little on modern graphics cards:

  • Anisotropic filtering (16x): Sharpens surfaces seen at an angle for almost no cost.
  • Texture quality: As long as you have enough VRAM.
  • Model and character detail: Small impact in most games.

Competitive games work differently

In Counter-Strike 2, Valorant or Overwatch 2 the goal is not the prettiest picture but high, stable frame rates and clear visibility. Some settings, such as shadows, are kept high for visibility regardless of cost, because seeing an opponent's shadow is an advantage. FPSmith's competitive mode makes that trade-off for you.
