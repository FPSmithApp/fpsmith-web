Frame generation: when should you turn it on?

By the FPSmith team · Published: October 20, 2026

How DLSS and FSR frame generation and multi frame generation work, what they do to latency, and when they help or hurt.

Frame generation can nearly double the number on your FPS counter. Unlike upscaling, though, it is not always a good idea. Understand what it does and it is very useful; ignore it and it can make a game feel worse.

How it works

The graphics card renders two frames normally, and frame generation inserts a predicted in-between frame built with AI or motion data. You see more frames on screen and motion looks smoother. Multi frame generation (RTX 50 series) can insert more than one frame between two real ones.

The key point: generated frames do not change how quickly the game responds. The game only processes your input on real frames. It even has to wait for the next real frame before it can show the generated one, so latency rises slightly. That is why latency reducers such as NVIDIA Reflex or AMD Anti-Lag are switched on alongside frame generation.

The golden rule: base frame rate first

Frame generation makes a game that already runs well look smoother; it does not rescue a game that runs badly. The widely accepted rule is to turn it on when you already get at least 50-60 FPS without it. Use it to go from 30 to 60 and the counter may say 60, but the game will feel as unresponsive as 30 and artefacts in the generated frames become more visible.

FPSmith takes this into account. For example, with an RTX 4060 in Cyberpunk 2077 at 1080p in "Cinematic experience" mode, the tool shows about 89 FPS with 2x frame generation, but on the same screen it notes that the real frame rate is about 51 FPS and that latency may be noticeable.

When to turn it on

  • Story and open-world games: Great results when your base frame rate is around 60 and you have a high-refresh monitor.
  • Slow-paced games: In strategy, simulation and flight games a small latency increase goes unnoticed. It even smooths processor-limited games, because the in-between frames are made by the graphics card.

When to leave it off

  • Competitive shooters: In Counter-Strike 2 or Valorant latency matters more than the FPS number. That is why FPSmith's competitive mode does not suggest frame generation.
  • Low base frame rate: If you start below 40 FPS, lower settings or use upscaling first.
  • Graphics memory at its limit: Frame generation needs extra memory and can cause stutters on 8 GB cards.

Which card supports what

DLSS frame generation runs on RTX 40 and RTX 50 cards, and multi frame generation on RTX 50 cards. AMD's FSR frame generation runs on a wider range of cards, with an AI-based version on the RX 9000 series. The game's own support matters too; FPSmith shows it for every game.
