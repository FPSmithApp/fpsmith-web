Game settings for gaming laptops and handhelds

By the FPSmith team · Published: October 20, 2026

Why laptop GPUs share desktop names but run slower, how power modes change your frame rate, and what matters on the Steam Deck and ROG Ally.

Gaming laptops and handhelds run the same games as desktops, but by very different rules. A graphics card with the same name can be noticeably slower in a laptop, and whether you are plugged in changes the result too.

Same name, different performance

An "RTX 4060" in a laptop is not the same as a desktop RTX 4060. Laptop chips run at lower power because of heat and power limits, and the power of the same model varies from one laptop maker to another. So it is normal for a game to run slower on a laptop than on a desktop.

When you choose Laptop on the System tab in FPSmith, the tool accounts for this and asks you for a power mode. Check which mode you play in using your laptop maker's app (such as Armoury Crate or Lenovo Vantage).

Play plugged in

Most gaming laptops sharply reduce graphics power on battery. For serious sessions, plug in and select the performance mode. If you must play on battery, capping the frame rate around 30-45 FPS extends battery life noticeably.

Heat and consistency

Laptops can heat up during long sessions and slow down. Keeping the vents clear, playing on a hard surface and using a cooling pad if needed stop frame rates dropping over time. A frame cap reduces heat as well.

Handhelds: Steam Deck, ROG Ally, Legion Go

Handhelds use integrated graphics and have very low power limits. The right strategy is different on these devices:

  • Play at the screen's native resolution. The Steam Deck's screen is 1280×800; anything higher is wasted work.
  • A 30-40 FPS target is reasonable. On a small screen 40 FPS can feel as smooth as 60 on a big monitor. The 40 Hz display mode on the Steam Deck OLED exists for exactly this.
  • Use upscaling. Options that run on any graphics hardware, such as FSR 3.1 and XeSS, are very valuable here.
  • Match the power mode to the game. In lighter games a low-power mode can almost double battery life.
  • Shared memory: Integrated graphics use system memory, so Medium textures are usually a good starting point.
