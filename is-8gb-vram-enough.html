Is 8 GB of VRAM enough in 2026?

By the FPSmith team · Published: October 20, 2026

Comparing the RTX 4060 with the 12 GB RTX 3060: which games hit the 8 GB limit, and what you can do about it.

It is one of the most common questions when buying a graphics card. Short answer: at 1080p, 8 GB still works in the vast majority of games, but in a good share of big releases from 2024 onwards you cannot use the highest textures. The long answer is more interesting.

What VRAM does

Your graphics card's memory (VRAM) holds textures, models and the data for the scene being drawn. When it fills up, the game has to move data to much slower system memory. The result is lower frame rates and, worse, sudden stutters and textures that load in late. Even when the average looks fine, the game feels choppy.

What we calculated: RTX 4060 (8 GB), 1080p

We ran all 100 games in the FPSmith settings tool on an RTX 4060 at 1080p with a 60 FPS target. The tool lowers texture quality by itself when needed to stay within memory. Even so, estimated memory use reaches the full 8 GB in 19 games, including Alan Wake 2, Hogwarts Legacy, Assassin's Creed Shadows, Doom: The Dark Ages and Crimson Desert.

The comparison below is more striking. In these games, the single biggest frame-rate loss at maximum settings comes straight from texture quality, in other words from running out of memory:

The interesting part: the RTX 4060 is the faster card. Yet in these games the slower RTX 3060 can use the highest textures simply because it has more memory.

What to do with an 8 GB card

  • Lower texture quality one step. At 1080p the difference between High and Ultra textures is usually only visible up close.
  • Keep ray tracing off. Ray tracing needs extra memory too.
  • Be careful with frame generation. It also needs a few hundred MB, which can tip a borderline case over the edge.
  • Use upscaling. A lower internal resolution eases memory pressure a little.
  • Close background apps. Browsers and streaming software use graphics memory as well.

If you are buying a new card

For 1080p, an 8 GB card still does the job, but over the next few years you will compromise on textures in more and more games. For 1440p and above, or if you plan to keep the card for a long time, we recommend 12 GB or more. If a model comes in 8 GB and 16 GB versions (such as the RTX 5060 Ti or RX 9060 XT) and the price gap is reasonable, pick the 16 GB one.

You can see how much memory your card will use in each game on the System tab of the settings tool.
