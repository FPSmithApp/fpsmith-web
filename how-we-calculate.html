How FPSmith calculates: our method and data sources

By the FPSmith team · Published: October 20, 2026

The model behind our frame-rate estimates: graphics card and processor scores, per-game load, setting costs, test sources and limitations.

FPSmith's recommendations and frame-rate estimates come from a model calibrated against independent hardware testing. This page explains openly how the model works, where the data comes from and where it can be wrong.

1. Hardware scores

We keep a gaming performance score for every graphics card and processor. The scores are derived from multi-game comparative testing by sources such as Hardware Unboxed and TechSpot. Processor scores use the Ryzen 7 9800X3D as the reference. For graphics cards we also record memory size, ray tracing performance and supported technologies (DLSS, FSR, XeSS, frame generation).

We also apply a memory (RAM) correction based on measurements from sources such as Igor's Lab: the effect of DDR4 and DDR5 speeds is calculated per platform (AM4, AM5, Intel generations), and X3D processors are treated as less sensitive to memory speed.

2. Per-game load

For each of the 100 games we modelled how demanding it is on the graphics card and the processor, how much memory it uses and what ray tracing costs. In 57 games the real graphics menu is reproduced one to one: setting names, levels and menu order. In 38 games every setting's cost is specific to that game; 19 Unreal Engine 5 games use the engine's shared settings structure. The remaining games use a general menu suited to their genre, and their guide pages say so clearly.

3. Choosing settings

The tool does not lower settings at random to hit your target. Each setting's frame-rate gain is weighed against its visual impact, and the setting that gains the most frames for the least visual loss is lowered first. This repeats until the target is met. The priorities change with the mode you choose: "Cinematic experience" favours image quality, "Competitive" favours high frame rates and visibility.

4. Drivers and updates

Estimates are made for a specific driver version, shown in the tool. When major game patches or drivers change performance we update the values. Unreleased games are estimated from official system requirements and beta testing and marked as "Estimated"; once a game is out we correct it against real tests.

5. Limitations

Like any estimate, ours is not perfect:

  • Different parts of the same game can perform very differently; our estimates target a typical gameplay scene.
  • In laptops the same graphics card behaves differently depending on the power its maker allows.
  • Background software, temperature and system settings affect results.

If an estimate is very different on your system, tell us; feedback goes straight into improving the model.

Independence

FPSmith is not affiliated with any game publisher or hardware maker and receives no payment from them. Advertising covers the site's costs and never affects our recommendations.
